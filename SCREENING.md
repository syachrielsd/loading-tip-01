# Screening — Loading Screen Gold: A Tip in 200 Characters

Judge criteria, in order: (1) IS a loading-screen tip for stressed devs AND AI
coding agents; (2) real laugh via a turn/punchline, not a groan pun; (3) lands in
a glance, every word working; (4) speaks to human dev and AI agent at once;
(5) fresh, no coffee/deadline/compile reruns. Scores are 1–5 per criterion.

## Candidates

1. [99] "Tip: while this loads, your AI pair is quietly rereading your code and judging your variable names."
   C1 5, C2 4, C3 5, C4 5, C5 5 = 24. Strong: specific image, agent silently
   judging you is a real turn. Loses to the winner on C2: its laugh is a smirk,
   the winner's is a snap.
2. [89] "Tip: if this build fails, blame the agent. It cannot defend itself, and it already knows."
   C1 3, C2 4, C3 5, C4 4, C5 4 = 20. Weak as a tip (joke wearing a "Tip:" hat);
   scapegoating the AI is also a familiar beat.
3. [96] "Tip: save your work before the agent does. It rewrote your function while you were reading this."
   C1 4, C2 4, C3 4, C4 5, C5 4 = 21. Good meta turn ("while you were reading
   this"), but no loading context and the advice is thin.
4. [91] "Tip: your AI copilot just paused to search the same error you did. You are both winging it."
   C1 4, C2 4, C3 5, C4 5, C5 3 = 21. Warm and dual, but "searching the same
   error" is a well-worn dev joke; fails C5 against fresher lines.
5. [104] "Tip: the loading bar is the only honest thing here. You and the agent both pretend to know what is next."
   C1 4, C2 3, C3 4, C4 5, C5 4 = 20. Wry observation, no real laugh, no
   punchline snap.
6. [97] "Tip: the agent finished your code, then spent longer doubting it than writing it. Sound familiar?"
   C1 3, C2 4, C3 4, C4 4, C5 4 = 19. A statement, not a tip; C1 is the most
   important criterion and it is weak there.
7. [101] "Tip: this screen is the one place you and the AI are equals. Both of you are just waiting and hoping."
   C1 4, C2 2, C3 4, C4 5, C5 4 = 19. Sweet, dual, but no joke in it at all.
8. [99] "Tip: your AI already wrote the fix. It is just waiting for you to stop hovering so it can show you."
   C1 5, C2 5, C3 5, C4 5, C5 5 = 25. The reversal: usually the human waits
   for the agent; here the agent waits for the hovering human. Tightened below
   so the punch word lands last.
9. [106] "Tip: the agent rewrote your function three times while you blinked. It still calls the first version best."
   C1 3, C2 4, C3 4, C4 4, C5 4 = 19. Statement, not a tip; punch is decent
   but the frame is weak.
10. [103] "Tip: while this spins, your agent is deciding how to tell you the bug was in the code you wrote at 2am."
    C1 4, C2 4, C3 4, C4 4, C5 3 = 19. "Code you wrote at 2am" is a familiar
    trope; loses on C5.

## Winner

Tightened from #8: the punch word ("hovering") now lands last, and "patiently"
reinforces the reversal (the agent is the patient one; the human is the
hovering one).

"Tip: your AI already wrote the fix. It is patiently waiting for you to stop hovering."
(85 chars)

Why it wins: C1 — genuine tip form, loading context implied by the wait, and
the advice (stop hovering) is aimed at the human while the agent is the
subject. C2 — the reversal is the punchline: the machine is done, the human
is the bottleneck. C3 — 85 chars, every word earns its place, punch word
last. C4 — both parties are in the joke and both feel it is about them.
C5 — no coffee, no deadline, no compile; the "agent waits for you" angle is
fresh.

## Runner-up

#1 (99 chars): "Tip: while this loads, your AI pair is quietly rereading your
code and judging your variable names." It lost on C2: "judging your variable
names" earns a knowing smirk, while the winner's reversal earns an actual
laugh, and the winner's punch word lands last where #1's punch sits
mid-sentence.
